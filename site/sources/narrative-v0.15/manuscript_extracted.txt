Abstract
Finite coherent dynamics can preserve a state difference while a restricted observation loses
access to it. We study this distinction on oriented tetrahedral chain carriers and separate re-
construction from finite record retention. A family-level gluing theorem shows that t tetrahedra
formed by fresh-vertex face attachment have a 15t-dimensional copied state and an (8t+7)-
dimensional normalized unique-simplex view. Under separate skew incidence dynamics with
nonzero exchange weights in each cell, snapshot and derivative together distinguish every copied
state. Any exact autonomous linear realization of the complete visible trajectory for arbitrary
initial states therefore needs 7(t-1) extra state coordinates. This requirement differs from lossless
residual storage for general gluings. We also derive conditioning and noise bounds, including
sharp two-cell and core-star gains, a fixed-norm ill-conditioning family, and an exact sampled-
readout contract. Exact spectra, observation examples and a closed-gluing control support the
argument, which is integrated with conditional reconstruction, capture and renewal contracts.
Standard Hodge, observability, projection-memory, synchronization and transfer methods are
credited. The result concerns declared finite models: it derives neither native configuration selec-
tion nor a calibrated physical metric, universal entropy-area density or gravitational equation.
Keywords: tetrahedral complexes; incidence dynamics; persistence; projective reconstruction;
finite memory; capture; renewal.

1. The problem: how does change produce things that last?
The world changes, yet it contains things we can recognize again. Signals travel, structures
survive encounters, and interactions leave traces. A theoretical account should explain how
these processes connect: how a structure is generated, how it preserves identifiable properties,
and how its behaviour becomes available to another process.
These are related questions with different mathematical requirements. Existence in a state space
is not a formation mechanism. Conservation of a full-state quantity is not guaranteed access
through a restricted readout. A successful transfer is not permanent memory. The purpose of
this manuscript is to make those distinctions part of one explanatory argument.
The organizing question is:
      Can constrained interactions explain the formation of persistent structures and read-
      able records within a common dynamical framework?
UD investigates tetrahedral packet complexes as a candidate architecture for that framework.
The word packet denotes a declared carrier and its state; it does not independently establish a
particle, a material substance or an isolated unit of physical energy. The theory must earn each
of those interpretations through a further map to observables.

The approach begins with a finite setting because its relationships and consequences can be
inspected exactly. A small carrier permits an explicit boundary matrix, an exact spectrum, a
complete observation map and a finite apparatus state. Larger claims should remain accountable
to these constructions.
Our narrative progresses through five questions. What can differ? What can interact? What
survives that interaction? What can a readout recover? What mechanism preserves the recov-
ered difference as a record? Formation and the physical bridge then become precise obligations
rather than unexplained transitions between equations.

1.1 Object, representation and observation
UD distinguishes the system being described from the mathematical model, numerical encoding,
and observation used to describe it. An exact expression and a finite approximation may refer
to the same intended quantity while carrying different information. Likewise, a complete state
and a restricted readout can support different conclusions about distinguishability.
The programme therefore asks which relationships survive changes of representation, which
distinctions remain accessible after coarse-graining, and what mechanisms make that access
persistent. Numerical convenience is a methodological choice whose consequences must be ac-
counted for. Continuity, discreteness, dimensionality, and scale invariance remain properties to
be established within a justified physical description.
This is the philosophical role of distinction in the present account: it makes access and persis-
tence explicit without equating the description with its physical referent. The methodological
governance addition [10] operationalizes this separation through exact specifications, transported
observables, and declared approximation errors.

1.3 Scope, contribution and relation to prior work
The paper studies finite models of distinction, access and retention. Its reviewable claim is that
these are separate mathematical obligations, illustrated by concrete tetrahedral calculations. It
does not establish that tetrahedra are the unique architecture of nature. The narrative motivates
the models; the declared assumptions determine their consequences.
Combinatorial Laplacians on simplicial complexes are established objects [11]. Discrete Dirac
constructions likewise predate this manuscript [12]. Knill gives the complete-graph Dirac spec-
trum, including the filled tetrahedron [18, §9.3]. The phase equivalence in Appendix D.7 iden-
tifies its spectrum with ours. The single-cell spectrum is established background. Weighted
simplicial Dirac constructions also predate this manuscript [19]. Norm conservation is a stan-
dard consequence of skew-adjointness.
Phase recovery from relative offsets belongs to the established synchronization problem [13].
The noiseless support-tree receipt in §9 gives a suﬀicient reconstruction under supplied nonzero-
support data and component references. It is not a new general synchronization algorithm. The
clock in §10 uses engineered perfect state transfer [14]; prefix dressing adds a supplied gate word,
not a new origin for the clock.
The main added result is the stacked-family memory theorem in §6.5, supported by a structural
rank proof. The selected glued spectra, five-cell comparison, two-cell observation obstruction
and retained interface provide concrete examples. General observability and projection-memory
principles are established background [20,21]. Results inherited from Papers I-IV are cited as
such. The present synthesis explains how their assumptions connect and where they do not.
We make no priority claim that these finite examples are absent from all prior literature; a
comprehensive novelty assessment remains a submission task.

2. Distinction: specify the alternatives before explaining their
evolution
2.1 State, observation and resolvability
Let a declared state space be 𝒳 and an observation map be 𝑂 ∶ 𝒳 → 𝒴. Two states 𝑥, 𝑦 are dis-
tinguishable under this exact observation when 𝑂(𝑥) ≠ 𝑂(𝑦). Under a noisy or finite-resolution
observation, distinguishability additionally depends on the output distributions, permitted de-
cisions and error criterion.
This operational definition makes the model’s alternatives explicit. It does not imply that phys-
ical existence depends on observation, nor that every state difference is already an irreversible
record. Distinction under a readout is relative to a specified map.
For real chain states, the observation might retain signed coordinates or only their squares.
For complex states, it might retain amplitudes, phases or bilinear receipts. These alternatives
preserve different information. A narrative that calls them all “information” without naming
the map would conceal the central problem.

2.2 A finite binary label register
On four vertices, the tetrahedral edge graph has three opposite-edge matchings:


                       𝑀1 = {01, 23},    𝑀2 = {02, 13},    𝑀3 = {03, 12}.

Assigning one bit to each defines Θ = 𝔽32 . Its eight labels separate into Hamming-weight sectors
of dimensions 1, 3, 3, 1. Vertex permutations induce an onto action 𝑆4 → 𝑆3 on the matchings,
with Klein-four kernel [1, §3].
The labeled product set 𝑆4 × Θ has 24 ⋅ 8 = 192 elements. Under the specified reference-edge
projection, its six edge fibers each have 32 elements. These are exact counts of declared objects.
They do not establish a physical coupling, a 32-stage history or a clock period. On the same
fiber one can supply an identity update, a transposition or a 32-cycle.
The binary register and the amplitude carrier below therefore have different roles. The first
organizes labels; the second supports continuous state evolution. A source law relating them
must be supplied or derived. Counting their coordinates does not provide that law.

2.3 Comparing event labels and composing a history
A receipt alphabet can distinguish event types without specifying which events can follow each
other. The additional working notes [24] motivate a conditional 44-label alphabet: eight fixed-
weight labels, eighteen onset labels and eighteen extinction labels, including address and parity.
It has 946 unordered comparisons of distinct labels. Only 400 pairs have even one ordering with
compatible incoming and outgoing Hamming weights. Full bit, frame and transport rules may
reduce this set further. These counts organize possible comparisons; an admissible event graph
and its update kernel remain separate obligations.
The onset/extinction block illustrates why numerical matches need typed provenance. Both
its same-address subset and its reverse-weight-step subset have 108 pairs, yet their intersection
contains only 36. They are different subsets. Neither their count ratio nor an assigned address
cost determines a winding increment. Appendix D.12 derives the counts and shows that a
winding or cost is recoverable from a receipt precisely when it is constant on each receipt fiber.

2.4 A reference kernel exposes what counting cannot select
Appendix D.13 supplies a declared fine-history model for that alphabet: a permutation of the
three pair addresses followed by no flip or one addressed flip. Its eight Boolean vertices admit
192 action-distinct edges. Separating the identity/no-flip null gives 44 active receipt labels plus
one null symbol. This all-open graph realizes every weight-compatible active receipt word, but
its admission rule is supplied rather than derived from the continuous carrier.
A complete normalized kernel can be written on this graph. Uniform primitive controls give
stationary weight populations (1, 3, 3, 1)/8. Other kernels with the same permutation symmetry
and positive action support realize every positive stationary weight distribution. Thus a valid
event alphabet and its symmetry do not determine formation preferences. The kernel’s source
and physical clock remain additional obligations; this is a prepared classical model, not the skew
flow of §4 [25].

2.5 When does continuous change count as an event?
A changing stock does not name its own event. A response rule must specify which change
matters and what persists after it. Appendix D.15 tests one explicit candidate: each opposite-
edge stock drives a memory bit with distinct on and off thresholds. Once those thresholds

                                                7
and an initial memory are supplied, the carrier trajectory determines a unique sequence of
simultaneous update batches. The gap between thresholds gives finite activity and a bound that
survives specified observation error.
The construction also reveals a constraint on the narrative. A symmetric vertex preparation
can drive all three pair addresses across a threshold together. Choosing exactly one address
then requires an additional reference, asymmetric state or stochastic mechanism; the coherent
trajectory alone does not distinguish one. The natural covariant batch is outside the reference
graph’s one-flip primitive alphabet, although its endpoint belongs to the composition group. This
candidate connects a carrier signal to explicit event semantics without deriving that semantics,
a native geometry update or a free physical measurement [27].
Reading an event requires another contract. Finite-rate threshold polling can miss a guard touch
even when the ideal response has a positive dwell time. Appendix D.16 supplies conservative
sampled guards and an explicit excursion/persistence condition under which every address-wise
event is confirmed within a bounded delay. Cross-address order needs additional timing sepa-
ration or uncertainty receipts: one simultaneous carrier batch can split across several detection
ticks. The distinction matters before a recorded order is used to compute a history-dependent
quantity [29].


3. Why tetrahedral structure enters
3.1 Relationships before metric
A state list does not determine which parts interact. A simplicial complex makes those rela-
tionships explicit through containment and shared boundaries. A filled tetrahedron includes
vertices, edges, faces and a cell. Its chain dimensions are


                           𝑓(Δ3 ) = (4, 6, 4, 1),       dim 𝒞(Δ3 ) = 15.

It is the smallest three-dimensional simplex. This supplies a compact architecture with a closed
two-dimensional boundary and several incidence levels. It provides a tractable setting for ex-
change within a cell and coupling between cells.
This explains why it is useful to investigate tetrahedra. It does not prove uniqueness among
candidate architectures or derive the dimension of physical space. A topological cell dimension,
a chain-register dimension and an apparatus Hilbert-space dimension are distinct objects.

7. Persistence and formation
7.1 State preservation does not imply attraction
Under the fixed skew flow, different initial states keep their separation. In particular, a state
cannot converge to a different fixed point: its distance from that point remains constant. Dis-
tance to an invariant linear subspace is also preserved, because orthogonal evolution preserves
both the subspace and its orthogonal complement.
This permits persistent modes and recurrence. It excludes attracting convergence to those tar-
gets in that particular full-state model. A projected observable can show apparent concentration
without establishing full-state attraction. Open or nonlinear dynamics requires a separate anal-
ysis.

A structure may form rarely and survive well, or form frequently and fail quickly. Its occur-

Proposition D4. The stock and rooted-tree receipt of §9 reconstructs the normalized state on

D.17.1 The declared signless tree map
The filled tetrahedron has a Hasse graph with 15 simplex vertices and 28 adjacent-degree con-

The graph is bipartite by simplex-degree parity. Let 𝑣 have value −1 on even degrees and +1
on odd degrees. Then 𝑆𝑣 = 𝐻𝑣 = 0. Connectivity gives ker 𝑆 = span{𝑣}, so every chord row

internal vertices and retains the two endpoints. Hence


                                  𝐻 = 𝐶𝑆,        𝐶 ∈ {−1, 0, 1}14×14 .

seven edges. A different tree can change 𝐶 and its error amplification. The graph argument is
standard incidence/path algebra; the certificate identifies this particular split, not a new general
reconstruction method.
