\section{An exact energy-preserving reflection}
For a node corresponding to simplex $i$ of size $|i|$, define the real unitary matrix
\[
J_i=\diag(1,(-1)^{|i|}I_{r-1}).
\]
For $r=1$ the unused block is absent. Define a map $\mathcal S$ on the full fixed-norm manifold by
\begin{align*}
U'_e&=-J_i\overline{U_e}J_j,& E'_e&=J_i\overline{E_e}J_i,
&&e=(i\leftarrow j),\\
q'_i&=J_i\overline{q_i},&w'_i&=J_i\overline{w_i}.
\end{align*}
\begin{lemma}[Reflection and soft parity]\label{lem:reflection}
$\mathcal S$ is an isometric involution, preserves $H$ and fixes the displayed critical representative. It preserves its gauge/species-phase orbit. At the displayed representative $p$, its differential on $\mathcal N$ $L=d\mathcal S_p$ is an orthogonal involution, acts as $-I$ on $K$ and fixes $h(k)$. Consequently
\[
T_3[k,h,h]=0,\qquad T_4[k,k,k,h]=0,\qquad T_5[k,k,k,k,k]=0.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Conjugation, fixed left/right unitary multiplication and a link sign are isometries of the declared product metric. The matrices $J_i$ are real and square to identity, so $\mathcal S^2=I$. In block node notation,
\[
D_{U'}=-J\overline{D_U}J,\qquad h_{U'}=J\overline{h_U}J.
\]
This proves matter-energy invariance. The four minus signs in a diamond cancel and its Wilson matrix is conjugate by the endpoint $J_k$ to $\overline{W_d}$; its real trace is unchanged. Electric squared traces and the constant mass term are unchanged.

At the background, $-\overline{-ib_e}=-ib_e$ in the active color. Adjacent simplex sizes differ by one, so the unused link block becomes $-(-1)^{|i|+|j|}I=I$. The real active matter is fixed. Node gauge $g_i$ is transformed to $J_i\overline{g_i}J_i$, and global species phases are conjugated, so the orbit is preserved. Hence its normal space is preserved as well.

A pure active critical phase tangent changes sign under $\mathcal S$. Orthogonal projection onto $\mathcal N$ commutes with $L$, so the intrinsic three-dimensional kernel is odd. The Hessian commutes with $L$. Tensor invariance gives $T_j[Lu_1,\ldots,Lu_j]=T_j[u_1,\ldots,u_j]$. In particular the fast covector $T_3[k,k,\cdot]/2$ is even; its inverse-Hessian image $h(k)$ is even. Each displayed tensor expression has an odd number of odd inputs and therefore vanishes.
\end{proof}
Because an isometry intertwines exponential maps, $f(Lu)=f(u)$ exactly. These are intrinsic jet statements; no numerical slice radius or freely acting final quotient is assumed. The map is used as an energy isometry. No symplectic or dynamical-reversibility assertion is needed.

\section{Cubic and quartic error bounds on the relaxation graph}
Write $u=k+z\in\mathcal N$ with $k\in K$, $z\in F$. Put $x=\|k\|$, $y=\|z\|$, $s=\|u\|$, $h=h(k)$ and
\[
w=z+h,\qquad W=\|w\|,\qquad H_h=\|h\|\le Dx^2.
\]
Assume $s\le R\le1$ and $DR\le1$. Then $W\le y+H_h\le2R$. Completing the leading fast terms gives exactly
\[
\frac12\langle Az,z\rangle+\frac12T_3[k,k,z]+\frac1{24}T_4[k^4]
=\frac12\langle Aw,w\rangle+q_{\rm rel}(k)
\ge\alpha W^2+\frac N{200}x^4.
\]
Here and below repeated powers in tensor brackets denote repeated arguments.

The remaining cubic is $T_3[k,z,z]/2+T_3[z,z,z]/6$. Substitute $z=w-h$. The potential constant $T_3[k,h,h]/2$ vanishes by Lemma \ref{lem:reflection}. The terms with two or more $w$ factors are bounded by
\[
\left(\frac{C_3R}{2}+\frac{C_3(2R)}6+\frac{C_3R}{2}\right)W^2
=\frac{4C_3R}{3}W^2.
\]
The remaining linear terms have absolute sum at most
\[
C_3Dx^3W+\frac{C_3D^2}{2}x^4W\le\frac{3C_3D}{2}x^3W.
\]
Young's inequality with $\alpha W^2/8$ gives the total cubic-error bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cubic}
\left(\frac\alpha8+\frac{4C_3R}{3}\right)W^2
+\left(\frac{9C_3^2D^2}{2\alpha}+\frac{C_3D^3}{6}\right)x^6.
\end{equation}
The last term accounts for $T_3[h,h,h]/6$ in absolute value.

The quartic with one $z$ is $T_4[k,k,k,z]/6$. Its $h$ part vanishes by parity. Its remaining part is bounded by
\[
\frac{C_4}{6}x^3W\le\frac\alpha8W^2+\frac{C_4^2}{18\alpha}x^6.
\]
The quartic terms with at least two $z$ factors are at most $(C_4/2)s^2y^2$. Since $k\perp z$,
\[
s^2y^2=x^2y^2+y^4\le34R^2W^2+10D^2x^6.
\]
Indeed $y^2\le2W^2+2D^2x^4$, $y^4\le8(W^4+D^4x^8)$, $W^4\le4R^2W^2$, and $D^4x^8\le D^2x^6$. Thus the total quartic error is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quartic}
\left(\frac\alpha8+17C_4R^2\right)W^2
+\left(\frac{C_4^2}{18\alpha}+5C_4D^2\right)x^6.
\end{equation}
The parity cancellations keep the constant losses at weighted order six. Bounding those terms only by an unrestricted fifth-order remainder would discard this improvement.

\section{The fifth-order jet and sixth-order remainder}
The order-five diagonal derivative bound $380N$ from [1], with real polarization factor $5^5/5!$, gives
\[
\|T_5\|\le\frac{380\cdot3125}{120}N<10000N=C_5.
\]
For $u_0=k-h$, parity leaves only the terms with one, three or five $h$ factors in $T_5[u_0^5]$. Using $Dx\le1$,
\[
\frac1{120}|T_5[u_0^5]|
\le C_5\left(\frac{Dx^6}{24}+\frac{D^3x^8}{12}+\frac{D^5x^{10}}{120}\right)
\le\frac{2C_5D}{15}x^6.
\]
Expand $u=u_0+w$. Since $\|u_0\|\le2x$, the single-$w$ term is bounded by
\[
\frac{C_5}{24}(2x)^4W\le\frac{2C_5R}{3}x^3W
\le\frac\alpha8W^2+\frac{8C_5^2}{9\alpha}x^6,
\]
where the last step also uses $R\le1$. The terms with at least two $w$ factors have coefficient sum $26/120$ and are bounded by
\[
\frac{26}{120}C_5(2R)^3W^2<2C_5R^3W^2.
\]
Altogether the fifth-order jet has absolute value at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fifth}
\left(\frac\alpha8+2C_5R^3\right)W^2
+\left(\frac{2C_5D}{15}+\frac{8C_5^2}{9\alpha}\right)x^6.
\end{equation}

For a unit-speed product geodesic, let $b$ be the total link speed and $c,d$ the two matter speeds, so $b^2+c^2+d^2\le1$. The same degree-four block Schur and Leibniz estimate as [1] gives the order-six matter derivative bound
\[
4N[(b+2c)^6+(b+2d)^6]\le4\cdot6^3N=864N.
\]
Every differentiated Wilson trace with six generator factors is bounded using two Frobenius norms and operator norms for the rest. Four edges per diamond, at most three diamonds per edge and $\kappa/r<N/25$ give a total magnetic bound $(2\sqrt3)^6N/25=1728N/25$. Electric derivatives above order two vanish. Hence globally
\[
|(H\circ\gamma)^{(6)}|<940N,\qquad
|\mathcal R_6(u)|\le\frac{940N}{720}s^6<2Ns^6.
\]
Here $\mathcal R_6$ is the Taylor remainder after degree five. Since
\[
s^6\le4(x^6+y^6),\quad y^6\le32(W^6+D^6x^{12}),\quad
W^6\le16R^4W^2,\quad D^6x^{12}\le x^6,
\]
the remainder is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sixth}
264N x^6+4096NR^4W^2.
\end{equation}
These global radial estimates do not require an assigned neighborhood for the final quotient.
