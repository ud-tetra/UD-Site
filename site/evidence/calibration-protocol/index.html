01

Timing feasibility is the first gate.

The proposed 0.2 ns budget is split into four 50 ps shares: reference epoch, path skew, within-run variation and clock drift. The drift share over the 8.286 ms record implies about 6.03 parts per billion fractional clock error under the declared linear-drift model.

Repeated RMS jitter alone cannot supply the required deterministic bound. The protocol requires applicable reference uncertainty, before/after checks and justified stability across every displaced sampling interval.

A 1 ns timing alternative fits the mathematical total budget, but fails the current 0.2 ns per-role requirement. It remains a separately identified design alternative; no frozen contract is replaced.

02

A conditional tail witness has measurable targets.

The proposed reference envelope has three lines at −25, 0 and +25 Hz with magnitudes 1/16, 3/4 and 1/16 V. Its in-band spectral variation is 7/8 V; a 1/1000 V tail leaves an amplitude floor of 0.624 V under the declared model.

A finite-mode theorem gives tail variation ≤√K times the sum of measured complex RMS and coefficient error, plus outside-model variation. The illustrative 100-mode witness needs 60 µV measured RMS, 20 µV total coefficient error and 200 µV outside-model variation to reach 1 mV.

Mode completeness, orthogonality, stationarity and outside-mode contamination remain independent premises. Source command values do not certify a nonlinear simulator’s output.
